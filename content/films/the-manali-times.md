## Voice-over

3:12 a.m. The music gives up before we do.

The flat is warm, loud, alive. Cushions on the floor. A glass tilted too close to the edge. Someone still arguing about which scene in Rockstar hits the hardest. We pull every new person into it like it's tradition now.

College first year feels far and close at the same time. We were strangers then. Now this place knows our fights, our silences, the way we take our tea.

Somewhere along the way, friends stopped being just friends. They became family. And without realizing it, their families started feeling like ours too.

At some point, I step out.

The night is colder than the room we left behind. Streetlights stretch across the road. A short rickshaw ride. Unfinished sentences.

A small wave and a side hug at a doorway that lingers half a second longer than it should.

Then I'm back alone. A text asking if I reached safely.

Inside, the party is thinning. Someone's asleep. Someone's booking a cab. Someone's already thinking about tomorrow.

And there it is again. That quiet burning sensation in my chest. Not loud. Just there. Like I want to hold the frame still. Like I want everyone exactly where they are.

But mornings don't wait. By sunrise, we'll all be back in our own spirals. Work. Responsibilities. Life pulling us forward.

The strange thing is, the party never really stops. Somewhere, it's still going. You're either in it, or you're the one walking back from it.

So I stand there for a second. And I take a picture of the mess.

So that I can stop time, because I am a magician.

And there's no difference between a storyteller and a magician.

## The night city

**Indiranagar, two or three in the morning, before sunrise.** Every store, pub and restaurant on the lane is pulling its garbage out onto the street. Shutters coming down. A few party groups waiting for a cab, or just sitting on the road. Somewhere someone is crying. Somewhere someone is happy. Nobody is looking at any of it.

## The morning city

**HSR Layout, seven in the morning.** All in black, a wired white earphone in, the bike out for a short ride, breakfast at a café. The same city, a few hours later, pretending the night never happened. He lives in both.

## The shot

A camera mounted on the back of a vehicle, moving slowly through the lanes before sunrise. It shows what the people on those streets don't see: what goes on in the hour the city cleans up after itself.

## The people

Three friends from college. They shared a flat then, and they share one now, in Bangalore, with jobs. The flat knows their fights and their silences. Their families visit, call, send food, and somewhere along the way became everyone's.

## Look and feel

A city film. Good faces, a lot of banter, and life: parties, offices, love lives, all of it happening at once in the same few streets. Warm inside the flat, cold the moment you step out of it.
